Tokenizer · fertility & the spread metric

Where does the score actually come from?

The grader scores spread, not compression: 1000 / (X₄ − X₁). Almost all of your gap is created before BPE ever runs — in the pre-tokenizer. Cross-word merges are a second, smaller lever, and your notebook already reaches for them. Here is the mechanism, measured on your four pages.

01

Fertility is tokens per faithful unit — and it has a floor

The metric counts a faithful unit as one run of letters/marks/digits, or one standalone punctuation char ([\p{L}\p{M}\p{N}]+ | [^\s\p{L}\p{M}\p{N}]). Your fertility X = T / D is how many tokens T you emit per faithful unit D.

The pre-tokenizer runs first and splits text into pieces the BPE merges can never cross. So every pre-token emits at least one token — the ratio pretokens / D is a hard floor on X that no vocab tuning can beat. The whole game is getting one faithful unit to become one pre-token.

02

The floor is being blown by a one-line bug

Your word branch is ?\p{L}+. Devanagari and Telugu vowel signs, virama, and anusvara are Unicode category M (marks), not L — so \p{L}+ stops at every matra and shatters each word into fragments. BPE can never rejoin them across the split:

Measured over the full pages, aligning the pre-tokenizer classes to the metric's unit classes collapses the floor — and therefore the spread — dramatically:

The reusable principle: make the pre-tokenizer's segmentation classes identical to the scorer's unit classes. One faithful unit should become exactly one pre-token, so BPE is free to compress it toward a single token. This is a correctness fix, not metric-gaming — and it's lossless and UNK-free by construction.

02

Cross-word merges: how your notebook already does it

The base tokenizer builds its stats from per-word pre-tokens — build_stats(raw_tokens=self.corpus) counts one word at a time, so a merge can never span a space. Your sentence_tokenizer cell changes the corpus, not the algorithm:

sentence_tokenizer = BPETokenizer(corpus=
      split_sentences_en(en_txt) +
      split_sentences_devanagari(hi_txt) +
      split_sentences_telugu(te_txt) +
      split_sentences_devanagari(sa_txt))
sentence_tokenizer.train_bpe_optimized(vocab_size=10_000)

Now each corpus item is a whole sentence, not a word. build_stats symbolizes the entire sentence — spaces included — into one byte string, so the greedy pair-merge is free to fuse across word boundaries. That is exactly cross-word / phrase tokenization: the space byte becomes just another mergeable symbol. Trained on your Hindi page to 1,500 merges, 12.4% of learned merges span a word boundary:

But there's a catch in the current build: training changed, encode() did not. encode() still calls pretokenize(), which splits on whitespace — so at encode time no pre-token ever contains an internal space, and every one of those 154 cross-word merges is unreachable. They occupy vocab slots and never fire. To realize the benefit you must encode on the same sentence segmentation you trained on (or add an explicit phrase-merge pass), keeping decode exact.

03

Which direction do cross-word merges move the score?

D is frozen by the metric; only T moves. A cross-word merge lowers T for whichever language it fires in, so it lowers that language's X. Because the score is pure spread, the sign depends entirely on which end you touch:

Apply cross-word merges to…Effect on its XEffect on spreadVerdict
English (current X₁, the min)↓ lowerwidenshurts
Sanskrit / Telugu (the max)↓ lowernarrowshelps

So phrase merges are a targeted Indic-only tool, used to pull the top languages down toward English — never applied to English, which would push the min lower and widen the gap. After the pre-tokenizer fix (§02) the ranking of languages flips — English becomes the worst-covered — so re-measure before spending slots here.

04

Why the small corpus makes this easy

These are four Wikipedia pages, and the grader trains and scores on the same text — it's closed-book. The vocabulary is tiny and repetitive, so a 10k vocab can nearly memorize the whole Indic lexicon:

05

Where the benefit comes from — ranked

  1. Align the pre-tokenizer to the metric's unit classes

    Word branch → ?[\p{L}\p{M}\p{N}]+; drop those classes from the punct branch. Retrain 10k. Spread 0.489 → ~0.03–0.08. Score ~12k–30k from one line. Lossless, zero-UNK.

  2. Make encode use the cross-word merges it trained on

    Wire encode() to the sentence segmentation (or a phrase-merge pass), targeted at the post-fix max language only. Reclaims the 154 dead merges. Marginal, and only after §1 tells you which language is the max.

  3. Equalize the residual by moving merge slots

    Use your per-merge usage table to trim slots from the min language and add held-out merges to the max, closing the last 0.02–0.05 of spread. Diminishing returns; do it last.

Do §1 first and re-measure before anything else. It very likely clears your 5k–10k target alone, and it changes which language is the outlier — which decides everything downstream.
