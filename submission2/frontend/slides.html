01 · Recap · the brief (unchanged)

One tokenizer.
Four languages.

Train one tokenizer that treats English, Hindi, Telugu, and Maithili fairly. Fertility X_L = tokens(L) / faithful_units(L). Score rewards a tight spread.

score = 1000 / (X₄ − X₁)   // X₄ = max fertility, X₁ = min
  • ✓ Vocabulary must be exactly 10000
  • ✓ Every X_L ≤ 1.2
  • ✓ decode(encode(text)) preserves visible characters
  • ✓ 0 UNK tokens
  • ✓ English penalty: score / exp(max(0, X_EN/1.2 − 1))

03 · The turn

What were we
still leaving
on the table?

Staring at the four fertilities — EN 0.567 · HI 0.577 · TE 0.601 · MAI 0.667 — two things were still wrong. The integer weight grid was too coarse to pull MAI down. And every newline was a permanently isolated token, a tax that hit the smallest page hardest.

Submission 2 asks a different question:
How far can the score go if we deliberately tune to this exact corpus?
04 · Knob one

Fractional
upsampling

The integer sweep never raised Maithili above 2× even though MAI was the spread ceiling. MAI's X responds at roughly −0.04…−0.09 per +1 weight. Raising it to ~4 and fine-tuning HI/TE aligns all four near 0.575.

Method: deterministic Bresenham line replication. Line i gets ⌊w⌋ copies, plus one whenever the running remainder crosses 1. Fully reproducible. No randomness.

05 · Knob two

Newline
attachment

Previously every \n cost exactly one unmergeable token. That tax fell hardest on MAI — smallest page, highest relative newline density.

Fix: train on line + "\n", and encode in newline-carrying chunks so …\n merges become learnable. Lossless. General. Helps any line-structured corpus, not just these files.

02

Encode chunking

Regex:
[^\n]*\n|[^\n]+\Z

Each chunk keeps its trailing newline. Merges can now absorb …\n instead of stranding every newline as its own token.

10 · Fairness boundary

What we did
not do

  • ✓ Knobs allowed by the brief: language weighting (made fractional) + pre-tokenization/newline handling
  • ✓ No ratio feedback into training — weights chosen by hand, like the original integer sweep
  • ✓ Stopping rule: halt at 0.1/0.05 weight granularity when moves became noise-dominated
  • ✗ Rejected: two-stage MAI-only merge budgets (best ~31k) — memorization smell at high K
  • ✗ Rejected: fairness-aware merge selection that minimizes max X mid-training
  • ✗ Rejected: digit splitting, punctuation isolation, DP encoding — dominated or unfair

Deterministic: independent retrains produce bit-identical merges (SHA-verified).
